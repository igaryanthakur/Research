## 7. FINDINGS SO FAR
F1. Dataset leakage. URL formatting reveals the class (scheme present in 8.3% of benign, 100% of defacement, 96.3% of malware, 26.4% of phishing). Same Random Forest on test: cleaned 0.8471, raw 0.9402, raw plus scheme flags 0.9466. Not yet checked whether another paper reports this.
F2. Preliminary SHAP vs LIME on V2 (500 URLs, 125 per predicted class, exact TreeSHAP vs LIME Tabular, top-5 of 36, threshold 0.50 fixed beforehand):
- mean top-5 Jaccard 0.1730 (95% CI 0.1571-0.1889); 95.6% of URLs below 0.50; mean Spearman -0.0101
- by class: benign 0.0487, defacement 0.2491, malware 0.3514, phishing 0.0430 (Kruskal-Wallis p = 4.17e-59)
- random chance for top-5 of 36 is about 0.082, so benign and phishing are below chance
- LIME mean local R2 0.1560 (poor fit)
- median time: TreeSHAP 224.19 ms (all 4 classes, 2,544 trees) vs LIME 60.85 ms (predicted class only)
Weaknesses: SHAP was in log-odds space and LIME in probability space; LIME fitted poorly. This is why it is preliminary.

## 8. DECISIONS ALREADY MADE (do not reopen unless I ask)
- V3 is final and frozen. 94.85% is accepted and reported as 94.85%, not "95%". No more training or tuning.
- The paper explains ONE model, V3. V1 and V2 are the route to it; their findings (F1, F2) are contributions found on the way.
- V3 is explained by URL-segment masking: cut the URL into segments, hide some, rebuild the URL, recompute all 348 features, call the complete stack, explain the stack's predicted class. SHAP and LIME both use this.
- Never perturb the 348 columns independently. Never explain a single base model and call it V3. Never present V2 explanation values as V3 explanations.
- Sem 3 package: a Streamlit web app (not a browser extension) that classifies a URL and highlights the segments that drove the result.
- The app cannot say "no HTTPS" as a reason; the model never sees the scheme.
- Known issue: V3 calls https://www.google.com phishing (88%) and https://mail.google.com/mail/u/0/ phishing (93%), and calls an obvious fake PayPal URL benign (91%). Likely cause: short bare-domain URLs in the dataset are mostly phishing. Handle with a small disclosed allowlist in the app, demo mainly on held-out test URLs, and report it as a limitation.

## 10. PROTOCOL CHOICES PROPOSED FOR STAGE 3 (ALIGNED WITH THE REVIEWED PLAN, NOT YET APPROVED AS A PROTOCOL)
- Segment: each unbroken run of letters/digits in the cleaned URL. Measured on 50,000 cleaned URLs: median 7 segments; 33.8% have 5 or fewer; class medians benign 7, defacement 12, malware 7, phishing 5.
- Unit cap: at most 10 units per URL; longer URLs have neighbouring segments merged into 10 groups, so there are at most 1,024 possible masks.
- Masking: remove the unit's characters, keep punctuation; one shared function for both explainers; masked strings are not re-normalized.
- Explain the stack's probability for its predicted class (both methods in probability space).
- SHAP: exact Shapley values from every possible mask (at most 1,024 model calls per URL); never sampled.
- LIME: the library's own linear fitter on the same kind of masks, sampling as in LimeTextExplainer, 1,000 samples, fixed seed, a weight for every unit.
- k = 3, minimum 6 segments, threshold mean top-3 Jaccard < 0.50.
- Chance level: random-ranking agreement for each URL's own unit count (0.37 at 6 units, 0.20 at 10). Explanations cannot be shuffled between URLs because segments differ.
- Class comparison is tested on agreement minus chance, because classes differ in URL length.
- Cost: model calls per explanation is the primary measure, wall time secondary (exact SHAP needs 64 to 1,024 calls, LIME always 1,000).
- Pilot gates: hard checks (features and predictions reproduce the saved ones, Shapley values add up); signal (hiding segments must move the output); LIME stability over two seeds; runtime. LIME fit (R2) is reported with a pre-stated interpretation and does not stop the experiment.

- A 6-segment minimum excludes about a third of URLs and at least half of phishing URLs. Report the excluded share.
